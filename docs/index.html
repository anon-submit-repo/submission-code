Text-space skill optimization

Learning to explore the skill‑edit space of frozen models

Every skill optimizer proposes then tests: a generator writes one candidate skill document, a validation gate accepts or rejects it, and the search over how to edit the skill stays implicit inside a single model call. MetaEx-Skill makes that search an explicit, learned object — a frozen learned explorer that reads the target's own failures, picks a population of edit operations from a menu, authors a full skill under each, and keeps the best through the same strict gate. No base generator seeds it, and the target model is never trained.

The explorer replaces the generator, not the gate

Prior methods differ only in their generator — reflect on failures, distill trajectory lessons, or evolve a population — and each hands one proposal to the same strict validation gate. We keep the gate and the frozen target untouched, and replace the generator with a learned explorer over an explicit menu of authoring operations.

STEP 1

Compact-fail evidence

The frozen target runs the training split with no skill (later, with the current best) and the harness scores every trajectory. We keep only the failures, drop the successes, and truncate each to a short window — a dense record of the recurring procedural errors, and the explorer's only input.

STEP 2

A population of operations

A frozen meta-controller reads that evidence plus a prose feedback history and samples Mpop operations from a six-item menu, without replacement. Each chosen operation is turned into a full skill authored from scratch, never an edit of an existing anchor.

STEP 3

Strict gate, then feedback

Every variant is scored on the held-out selection split and kept only on strict improvement over the current best — a tie falls back. Each operation's verdict (helped / regressed / gate-rejected, with its Δ) becomes prose the explorer reads next round.

One frozen model · three documents · three roles

The explorer is not a second model. Every role in the loop is the same frozen target reading a different document: the search policy is a document, the authoring step is a document, and the thing that ships is a document. Swap the document and you get a different agent — the weights never move. That is what makes the comparison compute-fair (the explorer adds prompts, not parameters), and it is why deployment needs no optimizer at all: of the three documents, only best_skill.md travels past the loop.

identity = the document in context

Both evaluation regimes are target-matched: the frozen target is also its own optimizer, so no stronger model ever enters the loop and a within-column difference reflects what is proposed, not who proposed it. The two explore-side documents are scratch state — they are rebuilt every round and never leave the optimization loop.

The operation menu — click an operation

Because operations are drawn without replacement, the population size is bounded by the menu: Mpop ≤ 6. Pushing past it forces duplicate or low-value operations — the same dilution that degrades PUPA at Mpop = 16.

Why a population clears a gate a single proposal ties out on

The gate filters a noisy estimate: with n selection items and per-item success p, the measured score carries σn = √(p(1−p)/n). A single genuinely helpful candidate that lands within σn of the no-skill score is tie-rejected, and the pipeline falls back to no skill — a lone greedy proposer has no second hypothesis to offer. If each independent variant clears the gate with probability q, a population clears it with 1 − (1 − q)Mpop, strictly increasing in Mpop. That predicts where the layer helps: most in moderate-headroom procedural regimes, and not at all where the gate rejects every candidate.

Drag the population size. The band is the real measurement noise for a 50-item selection split at p = 0.4; the acceptance probability is the exact expression above at q = 0.35 per variant.     The skill is a document, so it travels

    A skill is model-agnostic natural language, so we apply each method's artifact zero-shot to a different frozen target with no re-optimization. PUPA is the benchmark reported here because it is the one where the stronger target still has procedural headroom; near-ceiling benchmarks are already saturated and neither transferred nor locally optimized skills add much.

    Weak → strong · 8B skill applied to the 35B target

    Every method's Qwen3-8B skill goes to Qwen3.6-35B-A3B unchanged. The explorer's skill gives the largest cross-model gain, so what it learned is a transferable procedure rather than a target-specific artifact — and the whole result stays open-weights.

    Strong → weak · 35B skill applied to the 8B target

    The reverse direction is hard for everyone: a skill authored for a strong model can presume capabilities the weak one lacks. EvoSkill's skill regresses the 8B target by 6 points; the explorer's leaves it unchanged. Only these two author a non-empty skill on redacted PUPA at all.

    What drives the explorer — and what does not

    Two design choices carry the method, and one plausible-looking addition breaks it. All three are isolated on HotpotQA under the same setting and a matched round budget.

    Exploration-knob sweep · population × feedback mode
    3844score

    Population has an optimum. A single candidate is uniformly weakest, any population of two or more reaches the ceiling, and the gain saturates early — while the two failure-informative feedback modes win and full rollouts or mixed failure+success trail. Compact-fail is not a tuning trick but a prerequisite: every candidate conditions on the same evidence, so the more skills authored in parallel, the denser that evidence has to be.

    Anchor ablation · seeding the explorer on a base generator

    Anchoring on any base hurts. Seeding the explorer on EvoSkill, SkillOpt, Trace2Skill, or GEPA all underperform the anchor-free explorer — a heuristic seed injects its own biases and caps the ceiling. ★ marks an anchored run that still beats its own base generator: anchoring lifts the base, and caps the explorer.

    Why anchoring is unstable — every candidate that reached the gate

    The anchor is the base generator's selection-best member, so anchoring can be no more stable than that base's own member selection — which is itself decided on a small selection split where ties are common. Logging all 90 gated candidates per run shows the mechanism: each anchored run explores with higher candidate variance and a lower worst-candidate floor, without a better mean or a reliably higher best.

      Breadth: a frozen proprietary target, six benchmarks, four harnesses

      The head-to-head above is deliberately confined to public parts so it reproduces from pip. To show the method holds more broadly, we validate it on a frozen Claude Haiku 4.5 target across six benchmarks spanning direct chat, tool loops, vision, and an embodied environment, with a target-matched optimizer and an identical query budget per method. Every no-skill line is calibrated against the published tier for that model, so the splits are drop-in comparable rather than inflated.

      Test accuracy · all-Haiku, same setting

      The explorer takes or ties the best cell on all six. It wins the procedural regimes because the operation-menu population clears the strict gate exactly where a single proposal ties out and falls back to no-skill. Headroom is still necessary: absolute scores stay low where the frozen model is ability-bound rather than strategy-bound, and the win margin tracks that headroom.

      Anchoring ablation on the same six benchmarks

      Same conclusion as the open-weights regime, independently: the best anchored variant never beats the anchor-free explorer, and on the two tool-use benchmarks it collapses. Anchored runs do often beat their own base generator (★), which is why anchoring looks attractive until it is compared against not anchoring at all.

      “… a rigorous pre-generation validation phase … mandates an explicit lexical token counting mechanism before substantive text output generation begins … verifying both lower and upper bounds inclusively … list items are separated strictly by the requested delimiter and not by newlines or standard bullets.”

      Recurring length- and format-constraint failures become an explicit “count and check before you write” step — an added sanity check and formatting rule, synthesized into one procedure.

      Benchmark configurations · split seed 42, strict held-out gate

      “Regime” is the observed skill-leverage class. Skill optimization moves the score on procedural failures — where the target can succeed but follows the wrong procedure. It cannot help on capacity-bound failures, where no skill document has signal to exploit; a practitioner can tell the two apart before investing by measuring no-skill test accuracy with variance and classifying failures as near-miss versus structural.
